openTPU offers a readable, end-to-end open-source reference for how an AI accelerator works—from Python matmul kernels down to RTL—targeting developers who want vendor-independent inference hardware they can study or extend. The claim that AI agents designed the hardware themselves raises novel questions about AI-assisted chip design workflows.

**Linked source:** The openTPU GitHub repository describes a fully open-source AI inference accelerator—covering RTL (SystemVerilog), ISA, a bit-exact simulator, a kernel compiler, and host software—all developed by AI agents on a Xilinx Kintex-7 PCIe card. The project claims to run ten modern models (including LFM2.5, Qwen3/3.5, Gemma 4, Phi-4-mini, SmolLM3) with measured decode throughput ranging from roughly 4 tok/s for larger models up to 85.8 tok/s for LFM2.5-230M at 4-bit quantization, achieving 82–94% of DDR3-1066 DRAM peak bandwidth.

**HN discussion:** HN commenters reacted with a mix of curiosity about AI-designed hardware and philosophical speculation about recursive self-improvement, with one commenter framing the project as a potential milestone toward AI designing its own inference hardware.
